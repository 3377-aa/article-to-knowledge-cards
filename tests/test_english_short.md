# The Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is a time-management method built around fixed work intervals called pomodoros. One pomodoro is 25 minutes of uninterrupted work followed by a 5-minute break. After four pomodoros, the practitioner takes a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

The method's central claim is that frequent breaks protect sustained attention. Attention is treated as a limited resource that degrades with continuous use; short breaks allow it to recover before the next interval.

A second mechanism is task granularity. Each pomodoro is assigned a single task, and a task that exceeds one pomodoro is split into several. This forces vague work ("write the report") into concrete units that can be started immediately.

The method also includes a rule for interruptions: when interrupted mid-interval, the pomodoro is marked as invalid and restarted. The rule is deliberately strict, because the cost of an interruption is not the seconds it takes but the attention rebuild required afterwards.

Planning happens outside the intervals. At the start of a day, the practitioner lists tasks and estimates how many pomodoros each needs; at the end, actual pomodoros are compared with estimates to calibrate future planning.
