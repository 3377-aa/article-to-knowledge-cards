# Knowledge Cards · The Pomodoro Technique (source: test_english_short.md)

5 cards.

## Card 1: A pomodoro is a fixed 25-minute interval

- **Key point**: One pomodoro is 25 minutes of uninterrupted work followed by a 5-minute break; after four pomodoros, a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes follows.
- **Explanation**: The unit of the method is the fixed interval rather than the task, which makes the rhythm predictable and repeatable.
- **Example or self-test**: How long is the long break after four pomodoros? (15 to 30 minutes)

## Card 2: Frequent breaks protect attention

- **Key point**: Attention is treated as a limited resource that degrades with continuous use, and short breaks let it recover before the next interval.
- **Explanation**: This is the method's central claim: breaks are maintenance, not a reward for finishing.
- **Self-test**: According to the article, what happens to attention during continuous use? (It degrades)

## Card 3: Tasks are split to fit one interval

- **Key point**: Each pomodoro is assigned a single task, and a task that exceeds one pomodoro is split into several.
- **Explanation**: This forces vague work such as "write the report" into concrete units that can be started immediately.
- **Example or self-test**: A task estimated at three pomodoros is handled by splitting it into three units, one per interval.

## Card 4: An interrupted pomodoro is restarted

- **Key point**: When interrupted mid-interval, the pomodoro is marked as invalid and restarted, and the rule is deliberately strict.
- **Explanation**: The real cost of an interruption is the attention rebuild required afterwards, not the seconds lost during the interruption itself.
- **Self-test**: Why is the interruption rule so strict? (The cost is the attention rebuild, not the lost seconds)

## Card 5: Planning happens outside the intervals

- **Key point**: At the start of a day the practitioner lists tasks and estimates pomodoros per task; at the end, actual pomodoros are compared with estimates to calibrate future planning.
- **Explanation**: The estimate-versus-actual comparison is what lets the method improve over time instead of staying a fixed ritual.
- **Self-test**: What is the end-of-day comparison used for? (Calibrating future planning estimates)
